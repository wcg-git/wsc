01 — The Strategic Shift

Stop selling extra content. Start selling the industry.

Patreon stops being a tip jar for superfans and becomes the only place where fans, aspiring artists, songwriters, producers, and future executives learn directly from the people who shaped the records they grew up on.

"The music industry finally opened its office door. Learn the business from four people who run it, live, every month."

The faculty is already secured. Seven Grammy nominations between Chuck Harmony and Claude Kelly. An NAACP Image Award. A major label SVP office held by Phil Thornton. Sony Music label operations run by Tamone Bacon. Credits across Michael Jackson, Rihanna, Bruno Mars, Britney Spears, Whitney Houston, and Mary J. Blige. An Emmy nominated TV show on FOX SOUL, a Sirius XM radio show, and six produced seasons with a guest list the industry envies.

$25+

Education prices, not entertainment prices

Entertainment subscriptions cap near $15 because free alternatives exist. Education from credentialed instructors commands $25 to $250 because the alternative is a $30,000 degree or years of trial and error.

∞

Content that compounds

A masterclass filmed this month sells to every future member at zero marginal cost. Bonus clips decay in days. How Royalties Work is evergreen for years.

12+

Every month adds a class. By month 12 the catalog alone justifies the subscription, before a single live perk is counted.

Two tiers live on day one. The rest unlock as the Crew grows.

Four tiers, not six. A member deciding at checkout should face one easy question: fan access or education? Gating the premium tier behind a member milestone converts a small early base into scarcity, and the whole community works together to unlock it.

Confirmed on the August 31 call. The system batches: film once, publish for months. Filming staggers through the day to fit everyone's schedules, and the next shoot is 8 to 10 weeks out. Full hour-by-hour run-of-shows live in the production calendar spreadsheet.

Batch Day 1: The Masterclass Day

September 9. The first four roadmap modules: Start Here with Phil, The Song with Claude and Chuck, Handle Business While You Create with Tamone, and From Demo to Master with Chuck. Final hour: the 90-second trailer with all four hosts, plus a free preview per class for the social funnel.

Batch Day 2: The Series Day

Album Club episodes on Thriller and Miseducation. Producer Commentary on last season's most-viewed episode. Ask an Executive episode one. Then split units: Chuck films Producer Breakdown while Phil and Tamone film Contract Breakdown in parallel. Casual BTS capture throughout.

Production rule with zero exceptions: everything uploads native to Patreon. No external embeds, no password-protected players. That exact failure already cost a paying member in May.

04b — Production Standards

The rules every module ships under.

Set by the hosts on the August 31 review. These are quality gates, not suggestions.

Two minutes or less per segment

Talking head plus animation and B-roll. Studio B-roll pulls from session archives and AI-generated visuals. Every segment is visually engaging on its own.

Assume the viewer knows nothing

Layman's terms throughout. Acronyms are spelled out on first use with a quick visual definition. Elementary first, grad-level later. Downloadable keyword sheets accompany each section.

Tie back to the library

When a topic connects to an existing episode (a PRO discussion, a producer guest), a pop-up link points members to that episode rather than veering the lesson off course.

Legal review before release

All course materials pass through EJ or qualified counsel, with required disclaimers added. Examples stay general: no named artists, no specific situations that read as disparaging. Album Club gets copyright due diligence on every music snippet, with anniversary-pegged albums and rights-holder cooperation as the default posture.

06 — Success Models

The playbook is proven. WSC runs it with better credentials.

Four creator models shaped this plan. Each one contributes a mechanic that WCG will manage into the WSC system.

Model 1 — The Joe Budden Podcast, Patreon's #1 earner

Own the audience, ladder the access

Budden left platform deals to own his content and audience directly, reportedly clearing $1M/month on Patreon with a simple $5/$10/$25/$50 ladder, patron-only episodes, and a Discord community his members refuse to leave. His core insight: the community IS the product; the content is the invitation.

WSC application: the tier ladder mirrors this proven price architecture, then extends two premium rungs Budden never built, because Budden cannot break down a label release plan or play you the demo that became a Rihanna record. Chats live from day one; community becomes the retention engine.

Model 2 — Rick Beato, 5.7M subscribers

Teach free, build trust, sell the depth

Beato built the biggest music education audience on YouTube by giving away theory lessons, then productized depth: a $99 ear training program and paid courses. The funnel is free mass education into paid structured learning.

WSC application: every masterclass ships a free 3-minute preview to socials. The main show remains the free top of funnel. The full class, the library, and the worksheets live at $25. Later, evergreen classes can be repackaged as standalone $99+ courses for buyers who will never subscribe.

Model 3 — Top political and comedy podcasts (Chapo Trap House, TrueAnon)

Relentless cadence beats production polish

The highest-earning podcast Patreons win on one variable above all: a bonus episode lands every single week, on schedule, for years. Members subscribe to a rhythm, not a highlight reel.

WSC application: the weekly rhythm (masterclass, Album Club, breakdown, Ask an Exec) guarantees every member sees fresh value weekly at every tier, and batch production makes the cadence sustainable for four busy executives. The calendar is the contract.

Model 4 — Community-chat creators (The Girls Bathroom and peers)

The group chat is the churn killer

Creators running active member chats report roughly 50% monthly participation, and cite the chat, not the content, as the reason members stay. Patreon's own retention data says the same: community access correlates with sub-5% monthly churn.

WSC application: Chats opens with the relaunch, hosts drop in twice a week on a rotation WCG schedules, and every live event funnels back into the chat. Retention is managed as its own workstream, not a hoped-for side effect.

08 — How WCG Orchestrates

Talent secured. Machine managed.

The hosts own the content and the relationships. WSC's internal team owns posting. WCG owns the machine: calendar, pipeline, measurement, and the discipline that keeps a four-executive side project shipping like a media company.

The production calendar is the contract

WCG maintains the module backlog, books batch days, tracks every deliverable from Not scheduled through Published, and protects the weekly drop rhythm that the success models prove matters most.

The workload budget is the guardrail

6.5 hours per host per month at full build-out. Any proposed perk that adds recurring host hours without a price increase or milestone attached waits. This is how the system survives contact with four full calendars.

Unlocks are mechanical, not emotional

50 Studio Session members opens Platinum Records Club. A 15-person waitlist opens Hitmakers. Nobody has to make a judgment call under enthusiasm, and nobody overcommits early.

The scoreboard reviews quarterly

Thirty minutes, four hosts plus Aaron: tier mix, churn, play rates, workload actuals. Underperforming formats get fixed or killed before the catalog bloats. Wins get doubled.
